Risk Disclosure

FX Bot · Last updated 18 June 2026

This Risk Disclosure explains the key risks of trading the financial markets. Please read it carefully.

1. What trading involves and why it is risky

Trading means buying and selling assets — such as cryptocurrencies, gold and foreign-exchange pairs — to try to profit from changes in their price. Those prices move constantly and cannot be predicted with certainty, so every trade can end in a profit or a loss. When the market moves against a position you lose money, and when several trades or a difficult period go against you those losses add up. Trading is therefore high-risk and is not suitable for everyone. You can lose some, or all, of the money you commit. By trading the financial markets you take on these risks.

2. You can lose your entire capital

There is no protection against loss. In a worst case the value of a position or account can fall to zero. Profits from earlier trades can be given back by later losing trades, and a single bad period can erase gains built up over a long time. Trading capital is generally not insured, and no one can guarantee it.

3. No guaranteed returns

No one can promise or guarantee that trading will earn a profit. Past performance is not indicative of future results. Any figures, examples or illustrations used to explain trading are for explanation only and are not a forecast or guarantee of any specific outcome.

4. Market volatility

Crypto, gold and foreign-exchange markets can move sharply and unpredictably. Prices may gap suddenly, and values can fall as quickly as they rise. Market conditions can change without warning — including overnight, at weekends, or around economic and political events — and can affect every position at once.

5. Leverage and derivatives amplify losses

Where leverage, margin or derivative instruments are used, even small movements in the market can lead to disproportionately large losses, and those losses can happen faster than in unleveraged positions. The potential for higher returns always comes with the potential for larger losses.

6. Automated and algorithmic trading risk

  • Automated and algorithmic strategies can and do experience losing trades and extended losing periods.
  • Strategies calibrated on historical data may underperform or fail in unusual or unprecedented market conditions.
  • Technical issues — connectivity, outages, latency or third-party failures — can affect when and how trades are executed.

7. Execution, liquidity & slippage

Orders may be filled at prices different from those expected. In fast-moving or illiquid markets, stop-losses can be executed beyond their target level (slippage), which can increase realised losses.

8. Crypto-specific risks

  • Cryptocurrency transactions are irreversible — funds sent to an incorrect address cannot be recovered.
  • Network congestion can delay deposits and withdrawals.
  • The regulatory treatment of crypto assets varies by country and can change.
  • Stablecoins such as USDT carry their own risks, including the risk of losing their peg.

9. Only risk capital you can afford to lose

You should only commit funds that you can afford to lose entirely without affecting your financial wellbeing. Do not use emergency savings, borrowed money, or funds needed for essential living costs.

10. Not investment advice

This Risk Disclosure and any related content are provided for informational purposes only and do not constitute financial, investment, legal or tax advice.

11. Tax & regulatory responsibility

You are responsible for understanding and complying with the laws, regulations and tax obligations that apply to you in your jurisdiction. These vary by country and may change over time.

12. Seek independent advice

If you are unsure whether trading is appropriate for you, seek advice from an independent, qualified financial professional before committing any funds.
